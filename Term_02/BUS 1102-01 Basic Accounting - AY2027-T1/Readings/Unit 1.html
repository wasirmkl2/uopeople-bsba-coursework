Unit 1 Reading Assignment

As you go through the readings and watch the videos, consider the following:
  1. Why is accounting important to stakeholders?
  2. How can accounting information be applied?
  3. What is the accounting equation?
  4. How are financial statements prepared?

Read

  1. Franklin, M. Graybeal, P. Cooper, D. Bean, L., Burt, I., Carr, S., Collins, D. T., Coram, S., Creech, K., Czyzewski, A., Gauci, M., Greenman, C., Haselkorn, M., Irujo, C., Johnson, C., Khanlarian, C., Lukshaitis, T., Luna, D., Nantz, B., … Woolley, D. (2022). Principles of accounting, volume 1: Financial accounting. Open Stax. Licensed under CC 4.0.
    • View the Online Book
      • Read Chapter 1 - Role of Accounting in Society
        • Section 1.1 – Explain the Importance of Accounting and Distinguish between Financial and Managerial Accounting
          • This section explains the critical role of accounting in business. It begins by emphasizing the importance of accounting in monitoring financial performance and supporting decision-making processes. It also distinguishes between financial accounting, which involves external reporting for stakeholders, and managerial accounting, which supports internal decision making.
        • Section 1.2 – Identify Users of Accounting Information and How They Apply Information
          • This section explains that users of accounting information include internal stakeholders like management and employees, as well as external stakeholders, such as investors, creditors, regulators, and customers. Management uses this information for decision-making, planning, and controlling business operations. Investors and creditors assess financial health and make investment or lending decisions.
        • Section 1.4 – Explain Why Accounting Is Important to Business Stakeholders
          • This section emphasizes accounting’s significance to business stakeholders, such as investors, creditors, and management, by providing them with essential financial information to evaluate the company's financial health, make informed decisions, and ensure accountability and transparency.
      • Read Chapter 2: Introduction to Financial Statements
          • This chapter focuses on key financial statements and their components. First, they cover the description of the Income Statement, Statement of Owner’s Equity, Balance Sheet, and Statement of Cash Flows, emphasizing how these documents interrelate to provide a comprehensive view of a company’s financial status. Next, the objectives explain the definitions and distinctions between current and noncurrent assets, liabilities, equity, revenues, and expenses, with examples to illustrate these concepts. Finally, the objectives aim to equip learners with the skills to prepare an Income Statement, Statement of Owner’s Equity, and Balance Sheet, ensuring a practical understanding of these essential accounting documents.
      • Read Chapter 3: Analyzing and Recording Transactions
          • This chapter introduces the foundational concepts of debit and credit, which are essential tools for recording and analyzing financial transactions. Understanding these concepts will help you navigate the accounting cycle, apply the expanded accounting equation, and prepare accurate journal entries and trial balances. By categorizing transactions into assets, liabilities, equity, income, and expenses, you will build the skills needed to interpret financial statements and evaluate a company’s financial health from a stakeholder's perspective.
