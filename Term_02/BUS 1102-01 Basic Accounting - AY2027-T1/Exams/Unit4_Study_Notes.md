# BUS 1102-01 — Unit 4 Study Notes

Concept review for Unit 4: fraud, internal controls, and special journals.

## Fraud

- **Fraud triangle:** pressure (motive), opportunity, and rationalization. Internal controls mainly attack *opportunity*.
- **Asset misappropriation:** the broad category of employees stealing or misusing company resources. It includes:
  - **Payroll fraud:** ghost employees, inflated hours, unauthorized pay changes.
  - **Expense reimbursement fraud:** fake or inflated expense reports.
  - **Billing / disbursement schemes:** fake vendors, unauthorized payments.
  - Theft of cash or inventory.
- **Financial statement fraud:** intentionally misstating reported results, e.g., inflating revenue or hiding liabilities.
- **Bribery / corruption:** misusing influence in business dealings for personal benefit.
- **Fraud vs. error:** fraud is intentional; an error is an unintentional mistake.

## Internal controls

Policies and procedures that safeguard assets, keep records accurate and reliable, and reduce the risk of errors and fraud.

| Type | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Preventive** | Stop problems before they happen | Segregation of duties, authorization/approval requirements, physical safeguards (locks, passwords), pre-numbered documents |
| **Detective** | Find problems after they happen | Bank reconciliations, physical inventory counts, independent internal verification, audits |

- **Segregation of duties:** separate *authorizing*, *recording*, and *custody of assets*, so no single person can both commit and conceal fraud. Collusion is still a risk.
- **Documentation / audit trail:** a traceable record of each transaction from source document → journal → ledger → statements. It lets errors and fraud be traced and deters wrongdoing.
- **Limits:** controls reduce risk but cannot eliminate it (collusion, management override, human error, cost vs. benefit).

## Special journals

Each one groups a single recurring transaction type. That saves time and makes it easier to divide the recording work among staff. Column totals are posted periodically instead of line by line.

| Journal | Records | Typical entry |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales** | Sales on credit | Dr Accounts Receivable, Cr Sales (and Dr COGS, Cr Inventory under perpetual) |
| **Purchases** | Purchases on credit | Dr Inventory/Purchases, Cr Accounts Payable |
| **Cash receipts** | All cash coming in (cash sales, collections from customers, owner investment) | Dr Cash, Cr Sales / Accounts Receivable / other |
| **Cash disbursements (payments)** | All cash going out (paying suppliers, expenses, payroll) | Dr Accounts Payable / expense, Cr Cash |
| **General** | Anything that doesn't fit above: adjusting, closing, correcting entries, sales returns | Varies |

- **Subsidiary ledgers:** Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable subsidiary ledgers track individual customers and suppliers. Their totals must agree with the **control account** in the general ledger.
- **Key memory tip:** the word *credit* points to the Sales or Purchases journal; the word *cash* points to the Cash Receipts or Cash Disbursements journal.
